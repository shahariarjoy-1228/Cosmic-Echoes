### Engine and compatibility

The engine consumes the existing `music.js` events and preserves their pitches, identifiers, and composition-relative timing. Recursion still owns tempo scaling. Live playback and offline rendering share the same instruments, mix graph, and voice accounting.

| Instrument | Synthesis | Release after note end |
|---|---|---:|
| Starlight | Filtered sine with a quiet second harmonic | 220 ms |
| Echo | Softer filtered sine that preserves the mapped intensity | 160 ms |
| Nebula pad | Slowly attacked, low-pass sine for the mapped tonic and fifth | 600 ms |

One seeded, filtered noise loop supplies the atmosphere. A separate seeded stereo impulse feeds a shared reverb. The dry path uses gain `1 − mix`, and the wet path uses `0.65 × mix`. Conservative voice gains feed a compressor and master gain. Volume and mix adjustments settle within 85 ms of audio time.

The scheduler uses `AudioContext.currentTime`. It counts scheduled voices and their release intervals against a limit of **eight melodic voices**, with separate budgets for **two pads** and **one atmosphere source**. At simultaneous onsets, shallower notes get priority. Late or excess notes are reported with their IDs and suppression reasons.

Pause freezes the dedicated context and playback position. Resume continues the same timeline. Stop clears scheduled sources and the old effects graph and resets position. Replay starts the loaded composition again. Natural completion leaves the playhead at the endpoint. Offline rendering returns a stereo `AudioBuffer` at **44,100 Hz** by default, with up to **60 seconds** of composition and **four seconds** of tail.
